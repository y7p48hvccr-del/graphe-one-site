Help

Graphē Dictionary for Mac, iPhone and iPad

Looking up a word

Type in the search box at the top of the word list. Matching headwords come first, then words whose meaning contains what you typed. On the Mac, press ⌘F to jump to the search box. Choose a word from the list to read it. On iPhone, choosing a word opens it full screen; swipe from the left edge to go back to the list.

To look up a word inside an article, double-tap it (or double-click on a Mac).

Dictionary, Thesaurus and Wikipedia

The three pills at the top of the article switch how the chosen word is shown:

  • Dictionary — numbered senses with their source, pronunciation, related words, and optional examples, quotations and translations.
  • Thesaurus — Closest in meaning first (larger words are nearer), then groups by meaning, broader and narrower terms, related words and opposites. Tap any word to open it.
  • Wikipedia — the Wikipedia article for the word, inside the app. It needs an internet connection. See Wikipedia in another language.

Examples, Quotations and Webster's

On the Dictionary page, three small switches sit beside the pills. A filled switch is on; an outlined one is off.

  • Examples and Quotations show or hide those parts of each sense. They are included with the Maximum dictionary.
  • Webster's — when on, only the Webster's 1913 Dictionary (modernised) definitions are shown, across search, lists and pages. When off (the usual setting), Webster's definitions are hidden and everything else shows.

Text size

Tap the Aa button beside the pills and drag the slider. It runs from small but readable to large. On the Mac you can also use ⌘+, ⌘− and ⌘0 (normal size). Text size applies to the article, not the word list.

Topics

Tap the tag button to search within one subject, such as Computing. Type in the box to find a topic, and choose All Topics to search everything again. To hide a topic you never use from the list, slide it to the left (on a Mac you can also right-click it) and choose Hide. Hiding a topic only removes it from the list; its words remain in the dictionary.

Bringing a hidden topic back: open More ▸ Settings and tap the word Words, in the “In this dictionary” section, seven times in quick succession (less than two seconds between taps). A Hidden topics list appears, with a Show button beside each. It disappears again when you close Settings.

My Dictionary

Add your own words and meanings, such as jargon, names or anything you want to remember, in any language. Open More ▸ My Dictionary… and tap + (or Add a Word). Give the word, its type if you like, the language it is in, what it means, and an example if you want one. If you had already typed the word in the search box, it's filled in for you.

  • Your words appear first in the word list when you search, marked with a small pencil, and open like any other word. Your own wording is shown exactly as you typed it, with no spelling conversion.
  • Searching ignores capitals and accents, so “cafe” finds “café” and “uber” finds “über”. For Chinese, Japanese and Korean (and Thai), a single character also looks inside meanings.
  • To change a word, open My Dictionary and tap it. To delete one, swipe it to the left (or right-click it on a Mac) and choose Delete. Once you have set languages, a filter button lets you list the words in one language.
  • iCloud sync: if you are signed in to iCloud, your words sync between your Mac, iPhone and iPad through your own iCloud account. The newest change to a word wins, and a deletion reaches your other devices. The switch is at the bottom of the My Dictionary list. Syncing handles a few thousand words; beyond that the words are still saved on the device but stop syncing.
  • It doesn't need a download or an unlock, and nothing is sent to us.

Ontology: your journey and the word map

Tap the slate-blue Ontology button for a card with two views:

  • Journey — the words you have visited this session, in order. Tap one to go back to it. The ↺ button starts a new journey from the current word.
  • Word map — the current word at the centre with related words around it, joined by lines that show the relationship. If a word has several meanings, choose one at the top. Tap a surrounding word to make it the centre of the map; tap the centre word to open it in the article.

On the Mac, Go ▸ Back and Forward (⌘[ and ⌘]) move through the words you have opened.

Looking up a word from another app

Mac

  • Services menu. Highlight a word in any app, then choose Services ▸ Look Up in Graphē Dictionary. To give it a keyboard shortcut, open System Settings ▸ Keyboard ▸ Keyboard Shortcuts ▸ Services ▸ Text.
  • Dock icon. Highlight a word, then click the Graphē Dictionary icon in the Dock. The first time, macOS asks for the Accessibility permission, which lets the app read what you highlighted in the app in front. Switch it on in System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security ▸ Accessibility. Some apps, such as certain browsers, do not share their selection; use the Services menu there. The Dock-icon lookup is not available in the Mac App Store version, which uses the Services menu instead.

iPhone and iPad

Copy a word in any app, then open Graphē Dictionary. A short copied word is placed in the search box. iOS asks your permission to paste; to stop the prompt, choose Allow under Settings ▸ Graphē Dictionary ▸ Paste from Other Apps.

Settings: choosing a dictionary size

The largest dictionary has over 1.4 million headwords (words and phrases) and 2.1 million senses. The app includes the Essential dictionary, which works offline. Larger sizes are optional downloads: open More ▸ Settings, choose a size and tap Download. A downloaded size replaces the one you had, and an Update button appears when a newer version is available.

Graphē Dictionary comes with Essential. The larger sizes are optional downloads. You can also keep the files in a folder of your own, such as iCloud Drive or an external drive connected to your iPhone or iPad, choose that folder in Settings, and copy a size from it. The app keeps its own copy on the device, so the drive can be disconnected afterwards. The device still needs free space for that copy, and the file you copy from must fit on the drive: Maximum is 1.2 GB, so it will not fit on a 1 GB card, but Full (621 MB) will. Headwords include phrases and word forms.

Spelling: use More ▸ Spelling to show the English definitions in British or American spelling. British follows Oxford spelling: colour, centre, grey and analyse, with -ize endings such as organize. The choice applies to the English dictionary only, so it is hidden while a dictionary in another language is in use. Your own words in My Dictionary are always shown as you typed them.

Once one is downloaded, a small button with a flag appears beside the search box. Tap it to choose which dictionary to look words up in: English, or any of the languages you have downloaded. This is separate from the language the app itself is shown in, so you can, for example, use the app in English and look up French words. To remove one, tap Downloaded beside it in Settings and choose Delete Download. If it was the dictionary in use, the app goes back to English.

Wikipedia in another language

The Wikipedia tab opens the article in the language of the dictionary you are using. To read it in another language, tap the flag at the top right of the Wikipedia page and choose from Wikipedia's 300-plus languages. The word is still found in your dictionary's Wikipedia first, and then Wikipedia's own link to the same article in your chosen language is followed: look up “lighthouse” with Español chosen and you read “Faro”. If there is no article in that language, the original is shown with a note saying so. Choose Same as dictionary to go back. Your choice is remembered.

The app's language

Graphē Dictionary's menus, buttons and help are in English and 19 other languages: Čeština, Deutsch, Ελληνικά, Español, Français, Bahasa Indonesia, Italiano, 日本語, 한국어, Kurdî, Bahasa Melayu, Nederlands, Polski, Português, Русский, ไทย, Türkçe, Tiếng Việt and 简体中文. It follows your device's language. To choose another just for this app, open More ▸ Settings ▸ Language, then close and reopen the app. The flag on the More button shows the language in use. Topic names are translated too.
